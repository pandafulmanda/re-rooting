About the Seeds

Each packet has 20 seeds, a mix of zizotes (asclepias oenotheroides) and green (asclepias viridis) milkweed seeds. These seeds are hand-gathered by me, Amanda Shih, from the Chappell Hill area (~65 miles west of downtown Houston). Seeds were ripened / harvested from May through November of 2026.

Both milkweeds:

  1. Are drought and mow resistant once established

  2. Love sun

In my experience, the zizotes start very well without winter-sowing / CMS, whereas the green require winter conditions for a good germination rate. I am assuming that these seeds will be winter-sowned, and that both species should be able to start in such conditions.

Zizotes are the first milkweed to emerge in spring. In my observation, I have seen them re-emerge from established plants as early as February in Chappell HIll, so that they are ready for the earliest migrating monarchs in the spring to use as a host plant.

Both these milkweed can be found in Houston / Harris County as well (iNat Zizotes, iNat Green). I have just found them to be very plentiful in Chappell Hill and therefore, easier to collect seed from.

Caveats

There is a chance that I shouldn’t have packed these seeds together — where zizotes may truly germinate better in early summer conditions and green germinates better in the winter. Right now, I don’t know for sure. Online sources seem to suggest that zizotes are easier to start than green, but there’s not enough on how winter conditions specifically affect zizotes germination rate.

I’ve packed them together because while I have a pretty good idea which ones are which, I do not have 100% certainty as some of these I picked from pods where the plant had been mowed over. I have not tested all pod sources to confirm which are which.

If you would like to try to separate them, the seeds with a reddish-hue tend to be zizotes, and the dark brown ones tend to be the green. By contrast, green milkweed pods tend to have a reddish / purple-ish hue. I have not tested this enough to be 100% sure and haven’t quite found research online specific enough to these two Texas milkweeds.

My best germination rates with zizotes so far have actually been by water germination (soaking up to 48 hours) during the spring / summer / fall and then planting with radicals pointed down. I will be experimenting with starting zizotes myself this winter! I will likely have more zizotes seeds available to share next spring / summer if I find that they truly germinate better in non-winter.
